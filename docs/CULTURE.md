# Hikayat Kelip — Culture and Originality Guide

Every cultural reference in the game, what it means, how the game uses it, and the rules that keep it respectful.
Status: Phase 0 baseline, 2026-10-16. Update this file whenever new content references Malay culture, folklore,
architecture, language, music or another game.

## 1. Non-negotiables (from the GDD and the build prompt)

1. Never depict prophets, deities or real religious figures.
2. Never use religious texts, prayers, verses or rituals as mechanics, magic, UI, decoration or audio.
3. Never depict real royalty or real historical figures as characters.
4. No gore. Hits produce light sparks and ink motes; nothing bleeds; no bodies.
5. Villagers are never mocked. Cursed villagers are **freed, never killed**; a finished fight shows them turning back
   into people and fleeing or thanking Bayu.
6. Other games inspire principles only: no assets, names, characters, maps, UI layouts, music or distinctive art
   styles; no patented mechanics (e.g. the Nemesis System). Anything recognisable gets redesigned.
7. Dialogue in natural Bahasa Melayu and English, switchable, subtitled. **Rumi only**; no Jawi unless verified.

Why it matters: Malaysia's regulator has acted against a game judged religiously insensitive under the
Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 (GDD, [FMT](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2017/09/07/mcmc-wants-download-of-fights-of-gods-disabled)).

## 2. How the game handles sensitive areas

| Area | Rule in this game |
| --- | --- |
| Source of power and magic | The world's own rule (GDD): stories told aloud become kelip-kelip; buried stories rot into Kelam. All power — Kelip, the Pelita, Semangat, the finisher — comes from that rule. Never from prayer, ilmu (mystical knowledge), amulets (tangkal, azimat), incantations (jampi), bomoh/pawang rituals or sihir. |
| Curses | Harimau jadian are caused by Kelam (the buried story), never by black magic or a person's ritual. |
| Places of worship | None are shown or referenced (no mosque, surau or other worship building, no domes or minarets on the skyline). |
| Religious sound and text | No call to prayer in ambience, no religious songs (nasyid, qasidah, selawat, marhaban), no Quranic or Jawi calligraphy as decoration, no religious broadcasts on the village radio. |
| Religious phrases in dialogue | Avoided, even though they are common in everyday Malay (e.g. Assalamualaikum, Insya-Allah, Alhamdulillah, Ya Allah). Greetings use secular forms: "Selamat pagi/petang", "Apa khabar", "Jom". |
| Death and grief | Tok Wan's passing is mourned through memories, his book and the people he left; no funeral rites, graves or religious mourning gatherings are shown. |
| Books and their stands | Tok Wan's hikayat rests on a plain low table in his riverside pondok (DECISIONS D-028), never on an X-shaped folding book rest, which would recall the rehal used for the Quran. |
| Religious holidays | Festivals are invented and secular. Pesta Cahaya (Kota Kayu) is a harbour lantern festival and must not resemble Hari Raya or Malam Tujuh Likur (oil lamps lit in the last nights of Ramadan): no ketupat-light decorations, no Raya imagery. Avoid any confusion with Deepavali (also a "festival of lights"). |
| Attire | Everyday Malay attire is shown respectfully: baju Melayu, sampin, kain pelikat, baju kurung, kain batik, tudung for women who wear it. Religious headwear (kopiah, serban) is not used. The tudung is never a loot or cosmetic item. The GDD's cosmetic "headscarves" are men's folded headcloths (destar/tengkolok) in plain village folds, never royal styles. |
| Royalty | Kota Kayu's "carved palace" is a heritage carving hall; no ruler, royal family or court character appears; no royal regalia, royal yellow symbolism or real crests. |
| National and state symbols | No Jalur Gemilang, Jata Negara, state flags or crests, no real company logos (e.g. a wau design must not resemble any airline logo). |
| Animals | Village pets are cats (dogs are avoided as kampung pets out of cultural sensitivity). No pork or alcohol anywhere. The tiger is a national symbol and an endangered animal: Tok Belang is shown with dignity, and the 40-year-old hunt is framed as a grievous mistake — never a trophy or a triumph. |
| Ethnic portrayal | The setting is a fictional Malay island. If other communities appear (e.g. Kota Kayu traders), they are portrayed respectfully with no stereotypes or accents played for laughs. |

## 4. Language guide

- **Bahasa Melayu:** natural, warm kampung register; friends use *aku/kau*; Bayu calls his grandfather *Atuk* and his
  mother *Mak*; elders are *Tok*/*Pak*/*Mak*. Standard Rumi spelling (DBP); colloquial contractions (*tak, nak, dah,
  jom*) in speech, standard forms in UI. No dialect played for laughs.
- **English:** natural, not literal; keep game terms in Malay (langkah, tangkis, kuncian, buah, Pelita, Kelip, Cahaya,
  Semangat) with a one-line glossary hint the first time each appears.
- **Review:** BM dialogue gets a native-speaker read (Izaf) before each playtest gate.

## 6. Originality (other games)

| Influence (principle only) | What we take | What we must not copy | Our distinct form |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ghost of Tsushima | World-led guidance; duel openers | Wind-gust guidance look, its standoff presentation, UI | A synchronized firefly wave that blinks along mangroves toward the goal; a silat standoff read through footwork and bunga, not a sword quick-draw |
| Sekiro | Reading attacks; deflect pressure | Posture-bar UI, deathblow presentation | Tangkis staggers; kuncian *frees* (a restraint, never a kill); corruption bar instead of posture |
| Celeste | Forgiving platforming (coyote time, jump buffer) | Levels, art, character | Standard feel techniques, our own spaces |
| Titanfall 2 | Wall-run flow | Pilot moveset look, maps | Stilt-house and boat walls, momentum into the wau |
| Journey | Joy of gliding, wordless companion | Scarf/cloth look, desert art | Wau kite glider; Kelip speaks in colour and blinking |
| Trackmania | Medals, ghosts, instant restart | Track pieces, UI, medal art | Bunian medals (Gangsa, Perak, Emas, Bunian), original UI |
| Hades | Story revealed through repetition | Characters, art style | Hikayat pages returned by Pelita |
| Middle-earth (Nemesis System, patented) | Nothing | Adaptive rival hierarchies | Adi's ghost is a fixed friendly rival, not a nemesis system |

## 7. Review checklist (run for every new piece of content)

- [ ] No religious figure, text, prayer, ritual, building, sound, phrase or holiday imagery.
- [ ] Power, curses and magic come only from the story/Kelam rule.
- [ ] No gore; no killing of villagers; freed villagers shown with dignity.
- [ ] No royalty, historical figures, national/state symbols or real logos.
- [ ] Attire, food and animals follow §2.
- [ ] BM line reads naturally aloud; English line is natural; terms consistent with §3.
- [ ] Nothing recognisable from another game (§6).
- [ ] New terms added to §3 with meaning and source.
